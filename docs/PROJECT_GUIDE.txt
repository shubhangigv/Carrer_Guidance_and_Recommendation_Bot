==========================================================================================
1. THE PROJECT IN ONE PAGE
==========================================================================================

WHAT: A website where a student takes a short quiz and gets 10 careers from EVERY field (about 900
careers from the US O*NET database, adapted for India), each explained from the student's own answers,
with the skills to build, a step-by-step roadmap, Indian education routes, and a career chatbot.

WHY: In our survey of 87 students, 89.7% never had a career counsellor or were not helped by one,
and 23.0% are unsure they are on the right career path. Most career tools cover only IT or give one
generic list. Ours covers all fields, explains every match, and says honestly when it is unsure.

HOW (short):
  - Quiz answers -> Engine v3 scores every career (interest fit + readiness + strength fit).
  - A trained Logistic Regression ("field model") predicts which field students with similar answers
    and the same course aim for. Used when the student picks no areas.
  - Personalisation puts the student's chosen areas first, applies lifestyle filters, and pushes back
    careers that need the student's weak points.
  - Sentence-BERT matches the student's own words ("I like building websites") to careers.
  - Gemini chatbot answers questions only from our career records.

MAIN RESULT (survey, 81 students who named the field they aim for, cross-validated):
  The hybrid (ML field model + engine) puts the student's aimed field FIRST for 66.4% of students
  and in the TOP 5 for 71.4%. Random picking: 9.0% and 36.2%. Engine alone: 14.8% and 35.8%.

TECH: Python 3.13, pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn, sentence-transformers, FastAPI, MySQL (SQLAlchemy +
PyMySQL), plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript frontend, Google Gemini API, pytest (139 tests), Git/GitHub.


==========================================================================================
2. THE PROBLEM AND THE SURVEY
==========================================================================================

Survey: Google Form built by tools/create_career_survey.gs (9 questions). Closed for analysis at 87
responses (26 Oct). 52 more arrived later (139 on 29 Oct, 47 of them on 28 Oct); they are reported
separately (Section 14 I) and the website's field model trains on all of them.
Raw answers: data/raw/survey/responses.csv on the team PCs only (git-ignored, never shared).

The 9 questions:
  Q1 Year of study             Q2 Branch / field of study (10 options)
  Q3 Right career path?        Q4 Used a career counsellor?
  Q5 How much do you enjoy...  (6 interest rows = the 6 RIASEC types, 1-5)
  Q6 Rate your skills          (8 rows, 1-5, or "No experience" / "Can't assess")
  Q7 Rate your subjects        (8 rows, 1-5, or "Not studied")
  Q8 Which career field are you aiming for?   Q9 Would you use an AI career tool?

Key numbers (the 87 analysed responses, docs/results/survey/survey_summary.md):
  - 78 of 87 (89.7%) never used a counsellor, had none available, or were not helped.
  - 20 of 87 (23.0%) are not sure or do not feel on the right path.
  - 54 of 87 would use an AI career tool; 29 maybe.
  - 6 of 87 have not chosen a field yet ("Not sure yet").
  - Sample: 62.1% are 4th-years, 60.9% are engineering students (one college) -> a skewed sample.

How the survey is used:
  1. Problem statement (the numbers above).
  2. Evaluation: Q8 (aimed field) is the "label". Does the system put that field in a student's top-k?
  3. Training the field model (Logistic Regression) on the 22 answers + branch -> aimed field.


==========================================================================================
3. DATA: O*NET 31.0
==========================================================================================

O*NET = the US Department of Labor's occupation database. Version 31.0 (Aug 2026), licence CC BY 4.0
(free to use with credit; the website footer credits it). Files go in data/raw/onet/ (not in Git).

Facts (checked on the real files):
  - 46 tab-separated .txt files; 1,016 occupations; 910 have interest + skill + knowledge data and can
    be ranked (903 after the India filter removes gambling jobs).
  - Files used: Occupation Data.txt (code, title, description), Career Interest Types.txt (RIASEC),
    Essential Skills.txt (10 skills) + Transferable Skills.txt (25 skills), Knowledge.txt (33 areas),
    Job Zones.txt, Work Context.txt (outdoor/sitting), Related Occupations.txt, title files,
    Education file (US education shares).
  - Scales: IM = importance 1-5; LV = level needed 0-7; OI = interest 1-7; CX = work-context 1-5.
  - Job Zone 1-5 = how much preparation a job needs (3 = medium, 4 = about a bachelor's,
    5 = extensive: master's/PhD/medical). Students see Job Zone 3+ only.
  - Rows marked "Recommend Suppress = Y" (unreliable, e.g. tiny sample) are dropped on load.

India adaptations: short common titles (O*NET My Next Move titles), gambling jobs excluded
(config/india_exclusions.csv), Indian education routes, Indian job sites, cited Indian salaries only.

==========================================================================================
5. THE QUIZ (frontend/app/quiz.js) - one question per screen
==========================================================================================

Order and purpose:
  1. Areas you would like to work in - pick up to 3 of 17 (optional). Puts those careers first.
  2. Your course - 18 options, e.g. "B.Tech - Computer Science, IT or AI" (optional).
  3. 6 interests (1-5): Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising, Conventional (RIASEC).
  4. 8 skills (1-5, "I have never tried it", "Not sure"): Mathematics, Programming, Problem Solving,
     Research, Communication, Writing, Leadership, Technical/Practical Skills.
  5. Your 3 strongest skills and 2 weakest skills (optional). Fixes "rated everything 4".
  6. 8 subjects (1-5, "I have not studied it"): Mathematics, Physics/Chemistry, Biology, Computer
     Science, Commerce/Economics, Languages, Social Sciences, Arts/Design.
  7. Kind of workday: mostly desk/indoors, active/outdoors, no preference.
  8. Study more after your degree? no / yes / not sure.
  9. Where you want to work (city) - for job-search links.
  10. About you (free text, with idea chips like "coding and building apps").
Then a review screen. The 22 rated rows (6+8+8) are EXACTLY the survey rows, so the app and the
survey match (a test checks this). If a weakness was rated "Good", the quiz shows a message and
counts it as "Weak".

Why RIASEC: Holland's RIASEC interest model is the standard used by O*NET itself, so student
interests can be compared directly with each career's interest profile.


==========================================================================================
6. THE RECOMMENDATION ENGINES (knowledge-based, NOT trained)
==========================================================================================

Each quiz row maps to O*NET elements (ml/recommender_v2.py SKILL_MAP_V2 / SUBJECT_MAP_V2), e.g.
"Problem Solving" -> Complex Problem Solving + Critical Thinking; "Biology" -> Biology knowledge.
Student level on O*NET's scale: L = (rating - 1) / 4 x 7 (rating 1 -> 0, rating 5 -> 7).

ENGINE v1 (ml/baseline_recommender.py) - first version.
  Each part = Pearson correlation between the student's profile and the career's profile,
  rescaled (r + 1) / 2. Score = (0.30 skills + 0.25 interests + 0.15 subjects) / 0.70 x 100.
  Weaknesses: top 5 often all in one field; no handling of "not sure" answers.

ENGINE v2 (ml/recommender_v2.py) - readiness + honest ranges.
  - Interest fit: Pearson on RIASEC, (r + 1) / 2; flat answers -> 0.5.
  - Readiness: for each element, weight w = (IM - 1) / 4, met = 1 - max(0, LV - L) / 7;
    readiness = sum(w x met) / sum(w). Having MORE than needed never lowers it.
  - "Can't assess" -> a RANGE: low bound counts the unknown as level 0, high bound as fully met.
    Careers are ranked by the LOW bound, so a missing answer can never push a career up.
  - Display: <= 20% unknown -> one score; 20-50% or an important unknown -> a range + up to 3
    follow-up questions; > 50% -> score withheld (interest fit only).
  - Field diversity: at most 2 careers of the same field (SOC major group) in the top 5.
  PROBLEM FOUND: readiness was ~100% for almost every career for typical students (who rate
  themselves 4), so only interests decided the order -> odd results (coaches, fire chiefs).

ENGINE v3 (ml/recommender_v3.py) - USED BY THE WEBSITE. v2 + "strength fit".
  - Strength fit: Pearson correlation between the student's 8 skill ratings (and 8 subject ratings)
    and how IMPORTANT each of those rows is to the career. Question it answers: "are your strongest
    rows the ones this career relies on most?" Rating everything the same -> neutral 0.5.
    Strongest picks add +1, weakest picks -1 (fit only). Fewer than 3 usable rows -> 0.5.
  - skill part = 0.5 x readiness + 0.5 x strength fit (same for subjects).
  - match = (0.25 interest + 0.30 skill part + 0.15 subject part) / 0.70 x 100.
  - Weights were chosen by hand BEFORE seeing survey results ("pre-registered").
  - ENGINE_VERSION=v2 in .env switches the website back to v2.

Why knowledge-based (not trained) for the core: it works for any brand-new student with no data,
every score can be explained from O*NET, and it covers ~900 careers (the survey has 87 students).


==========================================================================================
7. THE ML FIELD MODEL AND THE HYBRID (the trained model)
==========================================================================================

File: ml/field_model.py. Model: Logistic Regression (scikit-learn), class_weight="balanced".
Pipeline: fill missing answers with the training median (+ "was missing" flags) -> standard scaling
-> Logistic Regression. Fixed standard settings; no hyper-parameter search (too little data).

Inputs: the 22 quiz answers ("no experience/not studied" = 0, "can't assess" = missing) and, when the
student gave a course, the branch (one-hot of the 10 survey Q2 options; each course maps to one in
config/course_areas.csv column survey_branch). Two versions are trained: answers only, answers + branch.
Output: the most likely survey field. Fields with fewer than 3 students are not learned. On the 87
analysed responses that is 5 fields (IT & Computing, Engineering, Healthcare & Medicine, Business &
Finance, Arts Design & Media). The website trains on ALL responses in the file: with 139 responses,
130 students in 10 fields (adds Management, Law, Life Sciences, Education, Social Work; only
Agriculture, with 2 students, is left out).

Training: in memory, every time the server starts, from data/raw/survey/responses.csv on that PC.
Never saved to disk, never committed. No file -> the app works without it (uses course -> areas).
USE_FIELD_MODEL=no in .env switches it off.

Why Logistic Regression: it was the best of the models compared (Section 14), it is simple,
fast, and explainable, and it suits small data.

THE HYBRID (what the website does when the student picks no areas):
  1. Field model predicts the field.  2. Engine v3 ranks all careers.
  3. The first 3 places = the best careers inside the predicted field that do NOT clash with the
     student's weak points; the rest = the engine's best others (with field diversity).
  The note on the page says it is the model's best guess and invites the student to pick areas.
  "3 places" was fixed before testing.

Why a hybrid: the survey study showed that interests (which the engine leans on) barely differ
between fields, while skills/subjects and the branch do; the ML model uses those well. The engine
then picks specific careers inside the field and explains them.


==========================================================================================
8. PERSONALISATION (ml/preferences.py, config/areas.csv, config/course_areas.csv)
==========================================================================================

Order of steps:
  1. Pool: Job Zone 3+ careers, minus careers the student marked "Not for me".
  2. Filters (only if chosen; each reports how many careers it removed; skipped if fewer than 30
     careers would remain):
       - "No further study" -> removes Job Zone 5 (master's/PhD/medical degree).
       - "Desk" -> removes careers outdoors about weekly or more (Work Context CX >= 3.5).
       - "Active" -> removes careers sitting more than half the time (Work Context CX >= 4).
  3. Score with engine v3.
  4. Weak points: a skill picked as weakest but rated 3+ is scored as 2 ("Weak"). A career CLASHES
     with the student if it needs a weak point (picked weakness, or a row rated 1-2 / never tried /
     not studied) more than 75% of careers do AND rates it important (IM >= 3). Clashing careers go
     last in every group, say why ("Heads-up: needs Programming..."), and never fill places chosen
     automatically (predicted field or course areas).
  5. Grouping: areas picked -> 7 "In your areas" + 3 "Also worth a look" (exploration).
     No areas but field model -> 3 from the predicted field + 7 others.
     No areas, no model, course given -> the course's areas.
  6. Reasons per career: "In an area you picked", "Linked to your course", "Uses your strength:
     Mathematics - needed here more than in most careers" (same 75% + IM >= 3 rule).

Areas: 17 (Computers & IT, Maths/data, Engineering, Science, Health, Business/finance, Management,
Sales/marketing, Law/government, Teaching, Helping people, Arts/design/media, Sports, Nature/farming,
Food/hotels/travel, Building/repair, Transport). Each = O*NET code prefixes, checked on real data.
Manager jobs (O*NET code 11) were re-sorted into their real industries (e.g. Fitness Coordinator ->
Sports, Health Services Manager -> Health).

None of this is part of the Hit@k evaluation (asking the area and then checking the area would be
circular). It is evaluated by the user test (Section 14, not run yet).


==========================================================================================
9. TEXT MATCHING AND CHATBOT
==========================================================================================

Text matching (ml/semantic.py): Sentence-BERT model all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (pre-trained, used as-is)
turns the student's text and each career's text (title, other titles, description) into 384-number
vectors; cosine similarity finds careers with similar MEANING ("drawing posters" -> Graphic Designer).
Only matches above a minimum similarity are shown (0.30 for Sentence-BERT; 0.08 for the TF-IDF
fallback used if Sentence-BERT is not installed). Too vague ("I love using laptop") -> "No confident
match" with ideas to write more. Career vectors are cached in data/processed/. The page always says
which method was used.

Chatbot (backend/assistant.py): RAG = Retrieval-Augmented Generation. For a question, it retrieves up
to 8 relevant career records (text matcher + the student's own matches), sends them with the question
to Google Gemini (default model gemini-3.5-flash, key GEMINI_API_KEY in .env), and Gemini must answer
only from those records. Without a key it runs in offline mode (lists matching careers). Unverified
education routes are flagged to the model as "not yet checked".


==========================================================================================
10. CAREER PAGES, ROADMAP, ROUTES, EXPLORE
==========================================================================================

Career page: description, "What you'd do" (first sentence of O*NET description), how to get there
in India (route + stream after Class 10 + US education shares + career ladder: easier jobs to start
in, harder ones to grow into), every important skill/knowledge (IM >= 3) marked have / build / check
yourself, salary only if cited in config/salary_india.csv (currently empty, so the page says no
verified salary yet), job-search links (LinkedIn, Indeed) for the student's city, similar careers.

Requirements and roadmap (ml/career_needs.py): skills to build ordered by priority = importance x gap;
first 3 "Start now", next 3 "Next", rest "Later". Roadmap = qualification -> skills -> experience ->
applying; ticked steps are saved (table roadmap_progress). Learning links are search pages only
(Coursera, YouTube, SWAYAM, NPTEL); specific courses only from config/courses.csv with a checked date
(empty for now).

Education routes (config/career_paths.csv, ml/pathways.py): 73 rows of step-by-step Indian routes
(e.g. Class 12 PCB -> NEET-UG -> MBBS) with official links. ALL WERE DRAFTED BY AI AND ARE MARKED
verified = no; the page shows "Not yet checked - confirm on the official site". A route counts as
checked only if every row is verified = yes. SHOW_UNVERIFIED_ROUTES=no hides unchecked routes (do not
use it until some routes are checked, or every route disappears).

Explore: 27 industries (config/industries.csv) and search by job title (incl. O*NET alternate titles).

Titles: 67 careers shared 22 short names (e.g. "Professor", "Engineer"); they now get specific names
("Professor (Engineering)", "Ship Engineers") so a list never shows the same name twice.

14. EVALUATION - EVERY EXPERIMENT AND FINAL NUMBER (87 responses unless stated)
==========================================================================================

All numbers come from scripts in experiments/; files in docs/results/. The report tables are
generated by experiments/report_tables.py -> docs/report/results_tables.md (never typed by hand).

A) Hit Rate@k (experiments/hit_rate.py, hybrid.py). 81 students who named an aimed field (Q8).
   "Hit@k" = the aimed field is among the student's top-k careers. Random = exact formula.
   Hybrids are cross-validated: the ML part never saw the student it predicts.

   Method                                         Hit@1    Hit@3    Hit@5   (95% CI of Hit@5)
   Random choice (expected)                        9.0%    24.1%    36.2%
   Engine v1                                      12.3%    25.9%    30.9%   (21.9-41.6%)
   Engine v2                                       7.4%    21.0%    32.1%   (22.9-42.9%)
   Engine v3 (pre-chosen weights, the website)    14.8%    29.6%    35.8%   (26.2-46.7%)
   Engine v3, weights tuned by CV                 14.6%    29.9%    36.5%
   Hybrid: ML field (answers) + v3                57.0%    57.0%    66.2%   (55.9-76.0%)
   Hybrid: ML field (answers + branch) + v3       66.4%    66.4%    71.4%   (61.0-80.3%)

   Hit@1 = Hit@3 for hybrids because the first 3 places all come from the predicted field.

B) Weight tuning (experiments/tune_weights.py): ~200 weight combinations, 5-fold CV x 5.
   Tuned: Hit@1 14.6%, Hit@5 36.5% vs pre-chosen 14.8% / 35.8% -> not better on both -> the website
   keeps the pre-chosen weights (rule fixed in advance).

C) ML vs engine (experiments/ml_compare.py): predict the aimed field. 77 students in 5 fields
   (fields under 3 students left out), repeated stratified 3-fold CV x 5 (3 folds because Arts has
   only 3 students).
     Majority baseline 35.1% acc / 0.104 macro-F1   Logistic Regression 60.5% / 0.527
     SVM (RBF) 58.4% / 0.412                       Random Forest 57.7% / 0.383
     Engine v3 top-1 field 14.3% / 0.199           Engine v2 7.8% / 0.079
     XGBoost 56.6% / 0.374 (run 29 Oct; no class balancing, unlike the others)
   (v2's macro-F1 was 0.083 on tanisha's PC: v2 has exact ties for 7 students and tie order
    differs between PCs; accuracy and every other number are the same.)
   (Favours the models: they choose among 5 fields; the engine ranks ~900 careers.)

D) Which inputs (experiments/model_features.py, same folds, rule fixed in advance):
     22 answers 60.5% / 0.527   22 answers + branch 69.6% / 0.636 (chosen)
     skills + subjects only 58.7% / 0.464   branch only 53.0% / 0.391

E) Survey study (experiments/survey_insights.py -> insights.md). Eta squared = share of an answer's
   variation explained by the aimed field (0 = none):
     Programming 0.52, Computer Science 0.41, Biology 0.28, Physics/Chemistry 0.17, Maths 0.15
     Interests: 0.01-0.04 (barely differ) -> explains why the interest-heavy engine misses.
   48 students aim for the field their branch leads to. 4.6% gave every skill the same rating.
   47.9% of all skill/subject answers are 4 or 5 (students rate themselves kindly).

F) Checks on O*NET with synthetic students (NOT accuracy; docs/results/summary.md, 910 careers):
   Self-match (a student copied from a career: is that career in the top 5?): Pearson v1-style 79.2%,
   weighted Pearson 67.0%, v2 64.9%, v3 94.7% (v3 partly favoured: built similarly).
   Weight sensitivity (v2 weights +/-10%): 95.5-97.3% of the top 5 stays the same.
   Unanswered questions: v2's range method never pushes a wrong career into the top 5 (naive method:
   0.70-1.00 wrong careers); after answering follow-ups the top 5 recovers 66% / 99% / 100%
   (1 / 2 / 3 hidden answers; with 1 hidden answer often no follow-ups are shown).

    The app still works; accounts are lost on restart.
  - "Text matcher is still starting": Sentence-BERT loads in the background (~1 minute); slower on

    $env:OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 before running Python.
  - Chatbot says offline: no GEMINI_API_KEY in .env (or no internet).
  - Chatbot "Gemini error 503 / very busy": Google's servers are overloaded. The app retries and then
    uses backup models (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.1-flash-lite; GEMINI_BACKUP_MODELS in .env);
    gemini-2.5-flash no longer works for new keys (29 Oct). If everything fails (busy, wrong key, no
    internet), the chat never shows an error: it answers with the matching careers from our own
    data and says why (gives up after 45 seconds).
  - No "Suggested field" on matches: no survey file on that PC, or USE_FIELD_MODEL=no, or the student
    picked areas (their choice wins).
  - Old results in the browser: press Ctrl+F5, or "Change my answers" -> "See my matches".
  - All routes vanished: SHOW_UNVERIFIED_ROUTES=no while nothing is verified -> set it back to yes.
  - Tests fail only once with memory errors: rerun after closing programs.

24. VIVA PREPARATION - LIKELY QUESTIONS, SHORT ANSWERS
==========================================================================================

(A shorter printable version with the demo script: docs/demo_and_viva.md)

Q: What problem does it solve?
A: Students lack guidance (89.7% of our 87 surveyed had no counsellor or no help). We recommend
   careers from all fields, explain each one, and show how to get there.

Q: Where does the career data come from?
A: O*NET 31.0 from the US Department of Labor (CC BY 4.0): ~900 rankable careers with interests,
   skills, knowledge, levels, job zones. We adapted titles, exclusions and routes for India.

Q: Is your main engine machine learning?
A: The core engine is knowledge-based: formulas comparing the student with O*NET profiles. It needs
   no training, works for any new student, and every score is explainable. The ML part is a Logistic
   Regression field model trained on our survey, combined with the engine as a hybrid.

Q: Why Logistic Regression?
A: We compared it with Random Forest, SVM and a majority baseline using cross-validation; it was best
   (60.5% accuracy, macro-F1 0.527 on 5 fields; 69.6% with the branch). It is simple and suits small data.

Q: What is RIASEC?
A: Holland's six interest types: Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising,
   Conventional. O*NET scores every career on them, so we compare directly.

Q: What is readiness / strength fit?
A: Readiness: do your levels meet the levels the career needs (weighted by importance)? Strength fit:
   are your strongest skills the ones the career relies on most (Pearson correlation)?

Q: How do you handle "I can't assess this skill"?
A: We give a score range (worst and best case) and rank by the lower bound, so a missing answer can
   never push a career up. We then ask up to 3 follow-up questions.

Q: How did you evaluate it?
A: Hit Rate@k: is the field a student aims for among their top-k careers? 81 students, compared with
   random. Hybrid: 66.4% at #1 and 71.4% in the top 5 vs 9.0% and 36.2% random. ML parts were
   cross-validated so no student's answer was used to predict themselves.

Q: What is cross-validation and why?
A: Split students into folds; train on some, test on the rest, rotate. It gives an honest estimate on
   unseen students instead of grading the model on data it learned from.

Q: What is macro-F1?
A: The F1 score (balance of precision and recall) averaged over fields with equal weight, so small
   fields count as much as big ones.

Q: Why is the engine alone close to random in the top 5?
A: Interests barely differ between fields in our survey (eta squared 0.01-0.04), and the engine relies
   on them. Skills, subjects and branch separate fields well, so the hybrid uses ML for the field.

Q: Did you tune the weights?
A: We tried cross-validated tuning; it did not improve both measures, so we kept weights chosen
   before seeing results. That avoids overfitting to 87 students.

Q: What is Sentence-BERT?
A: A pre-trained model that turns sentences into vectors so similar meanings are close. We match the
   student's own words to career descriptions with cosine similarity.

Q: What is RAG in your chatbot?
A: Retrieval-Augmented Generation: we first retrieve the relevant career records from our database,
   then Gemini answers using only those, which reduces made-up answers.

Q: How do you protect student data?
A: Passwords hashed with bcrypt, JWT login, students see only their own data, raw survey answers never
   leave the team PC or enter Git, the field model is never saved, no sensitive attributes are used.

Q: Limitations?
A: Small one-college sample; aimed field is a plan, not an outcome; US data adapted to India; routes
   still need checking; the user test is not run yet. (Section 16.)

Q: You got 139 responses - why does the report use 87?
A: We closed the survey for analysis at 87 before analysing, and fixed all rules on that. 52 came
   later; we ran the same evaluations on all 139 and report them openly. The task becomes 10 fields
   instead of 5, so numbers drop, but the hybrid still clearly beats the engine and random. The
   website's model trains on all 139, so it can suggest more fields.

Q: Future work?
A: Job listings, resume analyser, Indian occupation codes (NCO-2015 via ESCO), larger survey, user
   study, mobile/React app.

25. GLOSSARY
==========================================================================================

  O*NET        US occupation database used for every career's profile
  SOC code     Standard Occupational Classification code, e.g. 15-1252.00 = Software Developers;
               first 2 digits = field (15 = Computer & Mathematical)
  RIASEC       Holland's 6 interest types
  IM / LV      O*NET importance (1-5) / level needed (0-7)
  Job Zone     preparation needed (1-5)
  Pearson r    correlation (-1 to 1) of two profiles' shapes; rescaled to 0-1
  Readiness    weighted share of a career's required levels you meet
  Strength fit Pearson of your ratings vs the career's importance per quiz row
  Hit@k        share of students whose aimed field appears in their top-k
  CI           confidence interval (Wilson 95%)
  CV           cross-validation (train on some students, test on others)
  Macro-F1     F1 averaged over classes equally
  Eta squared  share of an answer's variation explained by the group (field)
  Hybrid       ML predicts the field, engine ranks careers inside it
  RAG          Retrieval-Augmented Generation (chatbot grounded in our records)
  JWT          JSON Web Token (login token);  bcrypt  password hashing
  Migration    SQL script that updates an existing database safely
  Pre-registered  decided before looking at results (prevents tuning on test data)
